## Panel-specific overlay

The transformed design is one specimen plate: the subject alone, turned to the side-on pose that identifies it best, laid along a diagonal across the middle with the one object it stands on or leans against following the same diagonal as a few broken dotted runs, shown whole and a size smaller than instinct, with more headroom above than below and quiet empty corners at the top left and bottom for the entry text. Everything is hand-made pen stippling in a single near-black ink taken from the photograph's darkest colour: shadowed masses are dotted almost solid, transitions thin out gradually, lit faces are left blank with only one row of fine dots along their edge, markings and boundaries read only from dot density, and small key details are a ring of dots around one solid dot. Dots are nearly equal in size, slightly irregular, never in a grid, never blurred. The whole design half is one flat pale ground tinted from the photograph's most distinctive colour, and the ink matches its temperature. Text is the same ink, small and well clear of the subject: a tiny entry number and a two-line, two-language common name in plain thin sans-serif at the top left, named after the subject's habit or this moment rather than its real name, and one letter-spaced italic serif pseudo-Latin name, a centred dot and a category word at the bottom centre. Reject a full-scene copy, a filled background, cartoon style, dots joining into outlines, grey wash or flat-filled shapes, gridded dots, any second colour, realistic colour illustration, sparse even dots that make the form vague, a pure white ground, text crowding the subject, and any part of the design touching the fold.

These overlay rules add to `references/soldier-runtime.md`. They never replace the source brief or the family runtime contract.

Write every selected final PNG directly inside one fresh task directory under `~/Desktop/xxd/xxd-panel-348/` (or the explicit `--out` root). Use collision-safe filenames; do not create source, mode, or size subdirectories and do not generate an automatic contact sheet.
